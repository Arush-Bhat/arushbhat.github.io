UNDERGRADUATE FIELD PROJECT · 2026

Testing the Effect of Inflow Metallicity on Molecular Gas and Star Formation in a One-Zone Gas-Regulator Model

This project examines whether changing the metallicity of gas entering a galaxy produces a measurable change in its molecular-gas content and star-formation history within a one-zone gas-regulator model.

I implemented and numerically integrated coupled ordinary differential equations describing gas and metal budgets. I first considered a metallicity-independent baseline model, then extended it using the Krumholz–McKee–Tumlinson (KMT) prescription to introduce a metallicity-dependent molecular fraction and star-formation rate.

The simulations compare different inflow metallicities while keeping the other adopted parameters and initial conditions fixed. Within the chosen model, the KMT extension produces differences in molecular fraction and a transient response in the star-formation history. These results depend on the model assumptions and parameter choices; they are not intended as universal predictions for real galaxies.
